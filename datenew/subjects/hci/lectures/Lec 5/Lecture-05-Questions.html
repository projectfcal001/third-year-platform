1. According to the lecture, what is design?
وفقًا للمحاضرة، ما هو التصميم؟
  • Achieving goals within constraints
  • Drawing aesthetically pleasing screens
  • Writing program code
  • Selecting color palettes
✅ الإجابة الصحيحة: A. Achieving goals within constraints
💡 ليه صح: التصميم هو تحقيق الأهداف ضمن القيود. الأهداف = الغرض (لمَن ولماذا يريدونه). القيود = المواد والمنصات والأجهزة والوقت والتكاليف والخلفية. باقي الاختيارات تمثل أجزاء سطحية فقط من عملية التصميم وليست تعريفه الكامل.

2. The golden rule of design states that you should:
القاعدة الذهبية للتصميم تنص على أنه يجب عليك:
  • Always follow form over function
  • Understand your materials
  • Iterate exactly three times
  • Keep the design as simple as possible
✅ الإجابة الصحيحة: B. Understand your materials
💡 ليه صح: القاعدة الذهبية للتصميم هي «افهم موادّك». في HCI ده معناه تفهم الحاسوب (قيوده وأدواته ومنصاته)، وتفهم الناس (نفسية واجتماعيًا وأخطاء البشر)، وتفهم التفاعل بينهم. الاختيارات التانية مبادئ تانية مش القاعدة الذهبية.

1. Define design as presented in the lecture, and explain its goals and constraints.
عرّف التصميم كما ورد في المحاضرة، واشرح أهدافه وقيوده.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Design is defined in the lecture as "achieving goals within constraints." Goals (the purpose): - Who the design is for (the intended users). - Why those users want it (their needs and motivations). Constraints (the things that bound the design): - Materials, platforms, and devices available. - Time available for delivery. - Costs and budget. - Background of the team and context. In short, design is the act of meeting the users' purpose within the limits of the available materials, time, money, and context.

2. Explain the golden rule of design and, for HCI, what it means to "understand your materials".
اشرح القاعدة الذهبية للتصميم، وماذا يعني في HCI «افهم موادّك».
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
The golden rule of design is: "understand your materials." For Human–Computer Interaction, this means understanding three things: 1. Computers — their limitations, capacities, available tools, and the platforms they run on. 2. People — their psychological and social aspects, including human error. 3. The interaction between computers and people — how the two sides influence each other. Without understanding these three layers, designers cannot make sound decisions about what is feasible, what users actually need, and how the two will work together.

4. What is a persona? Explain how it is used as a surrogate user during design.
ما هو الـ Persona؟ اشرح كيف يُستخدم كمستخدم بديل خلال التصميم.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
A persona is a description of an "example" user. Key characteristics: - It is not necessarily a real person — it is a synthesized description. - It is used as a surrogate user throughout design: the team keeps asking, "what would Betty think?" - Details matter — adding concrete personal details (name, age, habits, goals) makes the persona feel "real," which helps designers reason about design decisions empathetically. By anchoring design discussions around a persona, the team maintains a consistent user perspective rather than designing for an abstract or self-referential audience.

5. Discuss the purpose and characteristics of scenarios in interaction design.
ناقش غرض وخصائص السيناريوهات في تصميم التفاعل.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Scenarios are "stories for design." Their purposes: - Communicate with others (designers, clients, users). - Validate other models by "playing" the scenario against them. - Express dynamics: a screenshot shows appearance, while a scenario shows behaviour. Characteristics: - They are linear because time is linear and our lives are linear — but this also means they do not show alternatives. - A scenario is a step-by-step walkthrough that considers what users can see (sketches, screen shots), what they do (keyboard, mouse, etc.), and what they are thinking. - Scenarios are used and reused throughout the design process.

6. Compare local, global, and wider-level navigation design as presented in the lecture.
قارن بين تصميم التنقل المحلي والعالمي والأوسع كما ورد في المحاضرة.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Navigation design considers structure at several levels: - Local — looking out from a single screen. Includes breadcrumbs (showing the path through the web site hierarchy) and live links to higher levels. Designers should "beware the big button trap." - Global — the structure of the whole site and movement between screens. Used through hierarchical diagrams (tree-like) and network diagrams (different paths through the system). - Wider still — the relationship with other applications and the operating system. Concerns here include style issues (platform standards, consistency), functional issues (cut and paste), and navigation issues (embedded applications, links to other apps, the web). Local design keeps the user oriented on a page; global design ensures coherent movement across screens; the wider level makes the application a good citizen of the wider environment.

7. Compare hierarchical diagrams and network diagrams in global navigation design.
قارن بين المخططات الهرمية والمخططات الشبكية في تصميم التنقل العالمي.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Both are used in global navigation design, but they expose different structures: - Hierarchical diagrams — show a tree-like structure of screens. They are useful when the system has a clear parent–child relationship between screens (e.g., a settings tree or content categories). - Network diagrams — show different paths through the system. They are useful when users can reach the same screen through multiple routes (e.g., dashboards, search, and shortcuts that cross the hierarchy). In practice, designers often need both: the hierarchy to ensure a clear primary structure, and the network to recognize and design for the cross-links that real use will create.

8. Explain the basic principles of screen design and layout, including the available tools mentioned.
اشرح المبادئ الأساسية لتصيم الشاشة والتنسيق، بما في ذلك الأدوات المتاحة المذكورة.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Basic principles (the lecture's "ask / think / design" framing): - Ask: what is the user doing? - Think: what information, comparisons, and order are needed? - Design: form follows function — the layout should serve the task. The lecture groups the basic principles under: grouping, structure, and order; alignment; and use of white space. Available tools: - Grouping of items. - Order of items. - Decoration — fonts, boxes, etc. - Alignment of items. - White space between items. Decoration should be used sparingly — boxes to group logical items, fonts for emphasis and headings — but not too many, to avoid visual noise.

9. Discuss the alignment rules for different content types presented in the lecture (text, names, numbers, multi-column data).
ناقش قواعد المحاذاة لأنواع المحتوى المختلفة المذكورة في المحاضرة (نصوص، أسماء، أرقام، بيانات متعددة الأعمدة).
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Alignment rules differ by content type: - Text (English/European): align left, because that is the reading direction. Centered or right-aligned text is fine for special effects but hard to scan. - Names: usually users scan for surnames, so put surnames first (e.g., "Dix, Alan") to make scanning easy. - Numbers: think about purpose — for example, when comparing magnitudes, use alignment (often right alignment or decimal alignment) so that the largest value is easy to identify. - Multi-column data: scanning across gaps is hard. The lecture suggests two fixes — leaders (e.g., dotted lines connecting items to values) and greying (alternating row/column shading, possibly vertical too). The underlying principle is purpose-driven alignment: choose the alignment that makes the user's task easiest.

10. Explain the role of white space in design, using the lecture's counter example.
اشرح دور المسافة البيضاء في التصميم، مستخدمًا مثال الكونتر من المحاضرة.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
White space is not empty or wasted — it is an active design tool. The lecture identifies three uses: - Space to separate — keeps distinct items visually distinct. - Space to structure — communicates grouping and hierarchy. - Space to highlight — draws attention to the item that has space around it. The counter example makes the point visually: there are two ways to see a counter — "WHAT YOU SEE" (the items on top) and "THE GAPS BETWEEN" (the spaces). Both matter. A designer who only sees the items and ignores the gaps will produce cluttered, hard-to-read layouts, even when every individual item looks fine.

11. Compare aesthetics and utility. When can they conflict, and when can they work together?
قارن بين الجمال والوظيفة. متى يتعارضان، ومتى يعملان معًا؟
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Aesthetics and utility are not opposites — aesthetically pleasing designs can increase user satisfaction and improve productivity. However, they can conflict: - Mixed-up visual styles can be easy to distinguish visually but confusing overall. - A clean design with little differentiation can be confusing because nothing stands out. - Backgrounds behind text can look good but make the text hard to read. They can also work together — the lecture cites the design of the counter as an example where form and function reinforce each other, and consumer products where aesthetics are a key differentiator (e.g., the iMac). In short, beauty should support the task; when it interferes with reading, scanning, or distinguishing, it has gone wrong.

12. Discuss the bad uses of colour mentioned in the lecture and how to test for them.
ناقش الاستخدامات السيئة للون المذكورة في المحاضرة وكيفية اختبارها.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Bad uses of colour mentioned in the lecture: - Overuse — using many colours without a very good reason (children's sites are an exception). - Colour blindness — ignoring users who cannot distinguish certain colours. - Poor use of contrast — text and background colours that make content hard to read. The lecture also notes that older monitors had limited palettes, that colour tends to be overused simply because "it is there," and that colour should be used sparingly to reinforce other information (not to carry meaning on its own). How to test: - "Do adjust your set!" — adjust your monitor to greys only and check whether you can still read your screen. If the design depends on colour alone, it will fail this test, and you need to add redundant cues (shape, position, labels).

13. Explain localisation, internationalisation, and globalisation as presented in the lecture, with examples.
اشرح التوطين والدولنة والعولمة كما وردت في المحاضرة، مع أمثلة.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
Three related but distinct ideas: - Localisation & internationalisation — changing interfaces for particular cultures/languages. The practical tip is to use a 'resource' database instead of literal text in the source, because translations change text sizes and left-to-right order. - Globalisation — trying to choose symbols, colours, and conventions that work everywhere, so the same design can serve many cultures without modification. - Deeper issues — cultural assumptions and values, and the meanings of symbols. For example, a tick and a cross mean +ve and -ve in some cultures, but in others both mean the same thing ("mark this"). The lecture's takeaway: simply translating text is not enough. Designers must consider how the interface is built (resource database), what symbols they choose (globalisation), and the deeper cultural meanings those symbols carry.

14. Why is prototyping and iteration necessary in design? Describe the iteration loop.
لماذا النمذجة والتكرار ضروريان في التصميم؟ صف حلقة التكرار.
✅ الإجابة النموذجية:
The lecture states bluntly: "you never get it right first time." Designs rarely survive contact with real users unchanged, so iteration is necessary to find what is really needed. The iteration loop is: - Prototype — build a partial, experimental version of the design. - Evaluate — test it (with users, heuristics, scenarios, etc.). - (Re-)design — revise the design based on what was learned. - Repeat until "OK!" → done! Iteration lets designers get something concrete in front of users early, learn from the gaps between expectation and behaviour, and improve the design over multiple cycles rather than committing to a single, untested guess.
